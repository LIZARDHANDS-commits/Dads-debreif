# Pilot-model and control sources (phase B, the close-formation wingman)

Sources Patrick found on 13 Oct 2026 for how #2 flies close formation (TS-226, `docs/modules/turn-sim/spec.md` section 7b). Ideas and published formulas only: no code is copied from any of them and no library is added (AGENTS.md).

| File | Source | Licence | What we take from it |
|---|---|---|---|
| `mit-16-400-fall-2011-lecture-10-manual-control-ii.pdf` | MIT OpenCourseWare 16.400 Human Factors Engineering, Fall 2011, lecture 10 "Manual Control II" ([course](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/16-400-human-factors-engineering-fall-2011/)) | CC BY-NC-SA ([ocw.mit.edu/terms](http://ocw.mit.edu/terms)) | The crossover model: pilot and aircraft together act like a gain, a delay and an integrator; on a two-integration response (G to position, thrust to position) the pilot must act on the rate; delay 0.1-0.25 s; nested loops closed fastest first; the optimal-control pilot model's predictor and its trade of control effort against precision |
| `bhandari-csurcsia-2022-digital-implementation-of-the-pid-controller.pdf` | P. Bhandari and P.Z. Csurcsia, "Digital implementation of the PID controller", *Software Impacts* 13 (2022) 100306, [doi:10.1016/j.simpa.2022.100306](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.simpa.2022.100306) | CC BY 4.0 | A PID run step by step as a difference equation; the derivative filtered (an unfiltered rate amplifies noise); integral windup prevented; the "derivative kick" avoided (a jump in the target must not jolt the rate term) |
| `toochinda-2011-digital-pid-controllers.pdf` (this computer only, not in the repo) | V. Toochinda, "Digital PID Controllers", controlsystemslab.com, June 2011 | none stated | The standard and parallel PID forms and converting between them; the step-by-step (incremental) form for code. Its Ziegler-Nichols and relay auto-tuning are for unknown plants: not needed here, where the aircraft model is known |
| `digital-pid-controller-design-handwritten-notes.pdf` (this computer only, not in the repo) | "The Digital PID Controller Design", 13 pages of scanned handwritten lecture notes (2001), author unknown | unknown | The PID's three parts in words: proportional to the error, integral to remove a steady error, derivative as "an anticipatory action to reduce the overshoot" |
| (web page) | Claude A, "[For Beginners] A Complete Guide to Using PID Control and Tips for Tuning", note.com, 24 May 2025, [link](https://note.com/claude_a/n/n3b322beaccbc?hl=en) | web page | Tuning one gain at a time in widening steps (0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1, 2 ...), integral only if a steady error remains, derivative only if too slow; the names for what we are building: two-degree-of-freedom control (feed-forward plus feedback), cascade control (pace, then power), gain scheduling, anti-windup, the filtered ("incomplete") derivative |
| (web page) | MathWorks, [Crossover Pilot Model](https://www.mathworks.com/help/aeroblks/crossoverpilotmodel.html) (McRuer and Krendel, *Mathematical Models of Human Pilot Behavior*, AGARDograph 188, 1974) | web page | The crossover model's form and ranges: crossover 1-10 rad/s, delay 0.1-0.2 s |
| (web page) | S.S. Tohidi and Y. Yildiz, "A Control Theoretical Adaptive Human Pilot Model: Theory and Experimental Validation", [arXiv 2007.10216](https://arxiv.org/html/2007.10216v1) (2020) | web page | A pilot's total delay measured at 0.3 s (decision plus neuromuscular); feed-forward of the reference plus state feedback, the structure #2 uses |

None of them models formation flying: they are single-axis tracking tasks and general PID practice. The structure and ranges carry over; the echelon numbers are ours, tuned against Patrick's flying.
