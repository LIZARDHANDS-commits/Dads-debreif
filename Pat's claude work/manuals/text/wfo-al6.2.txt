Foreword 
Mar 25 
1. This edition of 15 Wing Flying Orders (WFOs) dated March 2025 supersedes all 
previous editions of 15 Wing Flying Orders. Please insert this foreword, as amended, 
to include the current Wing Commanders signature block. 
2. WFOs are promulgated in accordance with Royal Canadian Air Force – Flight 
Operations Manual, and are supplementary to Cdn Air Div Orders, B-GA-100-000/AA000 (Canadian Forces Flying Orders), and relevant Engineering Orders. WFOs are 
designed to standardize flying operations at 15 Wing. 
3. Pilots are expected to interpret these orders intelligently, bearing in mind that no 
order can legislate for every situation or replace judgement, sound management and 
supervision. 
4. All pilots shall be conversant with these orders and shall read and acknowledge 
understanding of these orders with a signature before commencing flying at 15 Wing. 
Thereafter, pilots shall read and acknowledge understanding with a signature quarterly. 
5. Authority for amendment of these orders rests with the CO 15 OSS. Suggestions 
for changes and inquiries shall be forwarded through normal channels to 15 OSS.

101 COMPLIANCE 
1. 15 Wing Flying Orders are promulgated in accordance with Cdn Air Div Orders 
and supplement those publications listed in WFOs Section 1, Article 101.2 a-l. 
2. All aircrew shall operate aircraft in accordance with the following Orders as they 
apply to their particular aircraft types in the following order of precedence: 
a. B-GA-100-001/AA-000, Flying Orders; 
b. Cdn Air Div Orders; 
c. RCAF Flight Operations Manual (FOM); 
d. Aircraft Operating Instructions (AOIs) or Flight Manuals; 
e. Flight Information Publications (FLIPs); 
f. For approved flight tests, C-05-020-007/AM-000 Flight Test Orders for the 
CF; 
g. 2 Cdn Air Div Standard Manoeuvre Manuals (SMMs); 
h. Canadian Forces Administrative Orders (CFAOs); 
i. Defence Administrative Orders and Directives (DAODs); 
j. Canadian Forces Air Command Manuals (CFACMs); 
k. Wing Flying Orders; and 
l. Unit Flying Orders.

pilots IAW the pre/post flight checklist detailed in Section 2 Chap 3-301.

211 UAV / Drone / RPA / MJPS RPAS Operations 
1. The 15 Wing Moose Jaw policy regarding the use of RPAs, camera ships, or any

in WSO 3000-8. In general, no person may operate a UAV inside the Moose Jaw 
control zone during airfield hours. Exceptions to this rule may be made on a 
case-by-case basis by the 15 OSS OPS O. The approval authority for UAV 
operations within the control zone outside of airfield hours is the 15 OSS OPS O. 
MOUs authorizing the Moose Jaw Police Service and Moose Jaw RC Aircraft 
Club authority to operate within the 15 Wing Moose Jaw CZ is contained in annex 
S and P respectively. 
212 Hair Length/Style Restrictions 
1. The 15 Wing Moose Jaw policy regarding the length or hair and style while flying in 
15 Wing aircraft for safety of flight is as follows: 
a. Shoulder Hair Length- If hair is to or on shoulder top length, it can remain 
free but should be checked during egress trainers or similar events should 
egress trainers not be conducted, to ensure that it will remain free of 
entanglement from any ejection seat, restraint systems and/or retraction 
reels; and 
b. Long Hair length – If hair exceeds the top of the shoulder length, it must be 
braided into a ponytail. The ponytail can, if desired, be put down the inside 
of the flying suit in order to prevent hair from being caught in retraction reels, 
but if left free, should be checked during egress trainers or similar events 
should egress trainers not be conducted, to ensure that it will remain free of 
entanglement from any ejection seat, restraint systems and/or retraction 
reels. 
213 Wearing of Facial Cosmetic Products 
1. 15 Wing aircrew are prohibited from wearing cosmetic products on the area of the 
face in contact with, or covered by, ALSE oxygen masks during use. This includes 
the use of products such as, but not limited to: lipstick, lip gloss, chapstick, 

Section 1 2-6 AL 6 
makeup foundations, and moustache waxes/products. These products shall either 
not be worn or shall be fully removed prior to wearing ALSE. 
2. Aircrew should consider using antiseptic wipes to fully clean the area of the face 
that will come in contact with oxygen masks prior to use, regardless of whether 
cosmetic products had been worn. After use, aircrew are responsible for 
conducting a thorough cleaning of oxygen masks with authorized supplies from 
their respective ALSE sections.

301 GENERAL 
1. 15 Wing has a mandate with a focus on pilot training and national community 
outreach for the CAF/RCAF. As a result, resources are limited and are usually already 
allocated to enable these two functions. Any operations not related to the daily mandate 
must be approved by the WComd in consultation with the senior staff, especially if it 
affects the public image of the RCAF. 
302 FAMILIARIZATION FLIGHTS 
Flight Approval 
1. The passenger approval authorities found within RCAF FOM 2.5.3.2.A, represent 
the lowest level of approval authorized for each category of flight and passenger. The 
Flight Authorization – Passengers form can be found in Annex N of these Wing Flying 
Orders. 
Requirements for Flights in Ejection Seat Aircraft 
2. Passengers shall not be flown in aircraft equipped with ejection seats unless they 
are in possession of valid documentation, which verifies: 
a. valid pilot medical category, or pre-flight medical examination by a 
qualified flight surgeon certifying fitness to fly as a passenger in an ejection 
seat aircraft (RCAF FOM, 2.5.3.2.C.2); 
b. successful completion of aero medical training or waiver by WComd (IAW 
Cdn Air Div Orders, Vol 5, 5-301, Para 18); and 
c. valid seat check. 
3. Prior to any flight, the pilot is to ensure that the passenger is within the weight 
limits for the ejection seat and fits safely in the cockpit. The pilot must also ensure that 
the passenger is able to perform the anti-G straining manoeuvre. 
4. In addition to confirming that the passenger has a valid seat check, the pilot shall 
review emergency egress procedures with the passenger prior to each flight. 
5. Waiver and Indemnification Form. Non-Canadian military personnel and all 
civilians participating in familiarization flights in ejection seat aircraft shall complete and 
sign the Waiver and Indemnification form found in Annex N of this document.

303 AIR DISPLAY APPROVAL PROCEDURES 
1. CEREMONIAL FLYPASTS. The WComd is the approving authority for local (within a 
50NM radius of CYMJ) military ceremonial fly-bys and local professional displays 
involving Wing assets. Once approved, 1 Cdn Air Div (A3 Special Events) shall be 
informed of all such events, including type of event, date and aircraft tasked to 
participate, no later than 14 days prior, in order to have them published in the Air 
Tasking Order. 
2. For all other approved air displays, the process as it applies to 15 Wing is as 
follows: 
a. A3 Special Events sends a staff check to 15 Wing through 15 OSS; 
b. 15 OSS forwards the staff check to the applicable units; 
c. if they can support the staff check request, the units initiate an Air Display 
Request (ADR) form (Annex Q), indicating support from the school Cmdt or 
Sqn CO, and submit it to 15 OSS. The unit also obtains the agreement of the 
contractor for use of their aircraft, through the normal cross-country process; 
d. for static-only displays the ADR is sent to the WComd for ‘Approval’. If the 
display includes a fly-by the ADR is sent to the WComd for ‘Support’ and

402 FLIGHT SAFETY OCCURENCES 
1. Reporting. After any flight safety occurrence, individuals involved shall report their 
occurrence immediately to their Flight Safety team by either phone call, in person or by 
filling out a CF215 or JIRA report before any other duties such as debriefs are complete 
to prevent loss of evidence. For more significant occurrences, individuals shall report to 
their Flight or Unit FSO immediately. The applicable UFSO shall, within 12hrs of the 
occurrence if practical or at the earliest opportunity, enter the incident into FSIMS. 
2. Quarantine. Following a FS occurrence all evidence related to the incident is 
immediately quarantined and is under the control exclusively of the Investigator In 
Charge (IIC) as per the Aeronautics Act. Items to be quarantined will be at the 
discretion of the applicable responding FS officer. Only the appropriate FS investigator 
can release quarantines. The aircraft is to be left as is except for essential requirements 
to make the aircraft safe. Aeronautics Act, section 14 (paras 7 to 9), permits 
investigators to prohibit or limit access to any area for any period necessary for the 
purposes of carrying out an investigation. Any evidence on airfield, on ramps, taxiways, 
or off airfield is to remain in place pending FS investigation and is not to be moved 
unless it creates an immediate safety hazard 
3. Away From 15 Wing. Should a FS occurrence happen away from 15 Wing, the 
aircraft(s) should remain in place until Flight Safety and the CoC has been contacted. 
Authority will be required to continue the cross-country or RTB as the situation dictates. 
Every effort should be made to collect and preserve evidence where possible. Such as 
but not limited to: 
a. Getting contact details of other parties if applicable; 
b. Obtaining fuel or oil samples; and 
c. Taking pictures.

1. When a hot brake situation after landing is suspected, the aircraft captain will 
declare an emergency stating “Hot Brakes” to ATC and turn on to one side of the

a. Tutor – The engine shall be left running. The engine shutdown signal will

704 AIRBORNE PHYSIOLOGICAL EVENTS. 
1. The aircraft commander (AC) will use their judgement in determining if an 
airborne physiological event is either an urgent situation and requesting a 
precautionary landing, or if it is a distress/emergency situation requiring an emergency 
landing. Some guidance to assist decision making follows as such: 
a. Urgent Physiological Situation (2-Bell Procedures Do Not Apply): 
i. Use of the RT “PanPan” is discretionary; 
ii. Aircrew may be feeling unwell/airsickness; 
iii. Expectation to be able to safely control aircraft and self-egress from 
 aircraft; and 
iv. No ambulance/EMS/ARFF response requested.

b. Emergency Physiological Situation (2-Bell Procedures Apply): 
i. Shall use RT “MayDay”, or plain language “declaring an

ii. Aircrew experiencing rapid decompression above 18,000MSL or

iii. Aircrew are unconscious / non-responsive, OR have experienced

MTCA DECONFLICTION PLAN 
BACKGROUND 
1. The intention of this deconfliction plan to improve airspace management within the 
MTCA to further mitigate the risk of Near Mid-Air Collisions (NMACs). 
2. Airborne aircrew shall not compromise flight safety in any way in an attempt to 
comply with these policies and procedures. 
LOOKOUT 
3. Lookout remains the most important factor in avoiding NMACs. All QFIs are to 
ensure that more emphasis is placed on teaching the importance and the method of 
executing an improved and more frequent lookout. QFIs are directed to make specific 
mention of the quality and/or frequency of lookout performed by the student, on 
progress cards, where appropriate, in an attempt to increase overall awareness. In 
addition, the UFSO and Flight FSOs will provide regular flight safety awareness 
briefings to all aircrew with specific emphasis on lookout included in this deconfliction 
plan. 
DUTY OPERATIONS DESK 
4. The Harvard Duty Ops Desk will annotate the area usage on boards in their 
respective Ops to provide an accurate and up-to-the-minute area overview. The 
annotation will consist of the call sign and number of aircraft for formations. No more 
than four (4) aircraft shall be operated within an airspace block. If formations are 
intending to break for fighting wing, double attack or conduct rejoins, they will be 
counted as individual aircraft otherwise the formation will count as one aircraft. 
5. In adverse weather conditions 2 CFFTS Ops will impose appropriate restrictions to 
the flying program, which may include limiting the number of aircraft launched or the 
type of missions to be executed. 
AIRSPACE MANAGEMENT 
6. When conducting flying operations in the MTCA aircrew should endeavour to 
utilize the full extent of the training area. Aircrew shall consider altitude management as 
part of individual deconfliction plans. This may include flying at irregular altitudes (e.g. 
11 300 vice 11 000 or 11 500 ASL) during Clearhood or Formation flying operations. 
When conducting IF training aircrew should endeavor operate at IFR altitudes (in 
thousands) appropriate for direction of flight, or vertically de-conflict with other aircraft 
working in the same area.

7. WATCHDOG ATC Service (CH 4 - VHF –119.0 ) is available to enhance

8. Aircrew shall call ‘Entering’ and ‘Exiting’ the Harvard areas as per Annex J of these 
orders on the applicable area frequency. Additionally, aircrew should inform Ops on 
exiting an area on CH14 (UHF) or CH16 (VHF) so that Ops can maintain an accurate 
area management plan. If required, aircrew may deconflict amongst themselves within 
an area, however aircrew shall use brevity to ensure that vital advisory calls (i.e. Spins) 
can be heard by all those using the applicable area. Aircrew shall momentarily 
discontinue in-flight instruction/conversation when calls are made on their area 
frequency as active listening-out is vital. 
DETERIORATING WEATHER 
9. When weather renders certain areas unusable, crews must call Ops on CH14 UHF 
to coordinate another area. Ops will advise the crews which areas are available and 
annotate their movement on the usage board. The maximum number per area shall 
always be adhered to and therefore, if no other suitable area is available, crews may be 
directed to RTB unless another callsign in the intended area can RTB instead. If certain 
altitude blocks become unusable, Ops can direct altered block altitudes however, Ops 
shall endeavour to not combine IF missions with CH or FM missions. 
TEST FLIGHTS WITHIN THE MTCA 
10. Due to the nature of test flights, Watch Dog will specifically advise all users upon 
entry into Harvard areas that a test flight is operating in the area in addition to other 
traffic. This will be further defined by a precise radial and altitude in the interest of flight 
safety if deemed necessary. The addition of “Test Flight” will prompt users to increase 
lookout vigilance in that area. This applies to PHOENIX Test Flights and 431 Sqn Test 
Flights. 
NAVIGATION FLIGHT WITHIN THE MTCA 
11. Aircrew conducting Navigation shall remain below 6000ft ASL for departure and 
recovery outside 15 DME within the MTCA. The operation of aircraft at altitudes 
appropriate to the direction of flight when in level cruising flight below 3000’ AGL is not 
mandatory. However, conditions permitting, it is highly recommended for all aircrew to 
fly at an altitude appropriate to the direction of flight (3500/4500’/5500’ ASL) when 
transiting to Hacks. Also every effort should be made to avoid any portion of VFR traffic 
pattern / IFR letdown areas.

IFR AIRSPACE BLOCK PROGRAM 
PURPOSE 
1. To improve airspace usage for 2 CFFTS IFR training during periods when 
military 1000-on-top flight is limited or not possible due to poor weather conditions in the 
MJ MTCA. 
PREMISES 
1. The operation of the program is dependant on the following premises: 
a. MJ Terminal will have radar available; 
b. 2 CFFTS will be either on or off the IFR Airspace Block Program; 
c. 2 CFFTS will schedule a minimum of 10 minute spacing between aircraft; 
d. Changes to normal MTCA airspace and procedures are minimized thereby 
reducing the personnel learning curve and ease the transition to / from the 
IFR Airspace Block Program. The Local Departure will be modified to 
accommodate entry into defined airspace blocks. All other airspace for 
the runway in use will be minimally impacted; 
e. Six Individual Work Areas available with a minimum of 30 degrees radial 
spacing with 5000’ vertical airspace block in the Low Level and 6000’ in 
the High Level for all aircraft to operate within. 
PROCEDURE 
1. Initiation of IFR Airspace Block Program. 2 CFFTS will advise Terminal that they 
are going on the IFR Airspace Block Program. Aircraft intending to conduct operations 
under the IFR Airspace Block Program will then launch as per Premise C. If IFR 
Airspace Block Program initiation does not occur at the start of flying operations (no 
aircraft airborne) then transition to the program will occur once Terminal has confirmed 
all airborne aircraft are no longer conducting Military 1000-on-top operations. Once all 
aircraft are either back in the VFR traffic pattern, have been assigned an Individual 
Work Area or are on IFR approach, Terminal will make a guard transmission and advise 
AIS, 2 CFFTS Ops and Tower that the IFR Airspace Block Program is now in effect. 
2. Individual Work Area (IWA). Three low level and three high level IWAs are 
available for use on each runway. IWA locations and usage will not affect holding 
airspace, approach profiles, and minimally impact the Local Departure profile. The 
Snowbird Area will not be affected.
