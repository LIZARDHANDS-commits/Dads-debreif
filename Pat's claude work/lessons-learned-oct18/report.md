## 1. The answer

- **What was read:** all 58 project threads and the project chat (11,973 messages, 2,160 of them yours, 29 Sep to 18 Oct), 217 process documents out of 1,142 scanned (repo, project files, team memory), and the git history of the rule book and skills.
- **What came out:** 821 candidate lessons, merged into 207 distinct lessons, folded here into **7 foundation questions and 34 proposals**. The full 207-row register is in the project files (`lessons-learned-oct18/register.csv`).
- **The biggest lesson is about rules themselves.** 52 of the 207 lessons are already in the rule book, yet 101 of the 136 lessons that repeat one of the ten 3 Oct lessons happened again after 3 Oct. Writing a rule down did not stop it. What worked was a mechanism: the testing lines pasted into every brief, the build stamping the version, scripts checking quotes, fresh threads started from a handover, and cards for questions.
- **The rule book would quadruple if every proposal landed as written** (9,138 words of proposed wording against 3,014 words in `AGENTS.md` today). So the proposals below are folded, and two foundation questions (F5, F6) are about keeping briefs and rule files short.
- **What is not covered:** your PC (task-observer log, Antigravity and local Claude Code sessions), other projects, and anything before 29 Sep. Most of the work from 13 to 17 Oct happened on your PC, so the cloud threads hold almost nothing for 7 to 9 and 13 to 16 Oct.

## 6. How this was done, and what is unseen

- **Pipeline:** scripts dumped every thread and the chat and filtered them; 15 Haiku extractors wrote 821 candidates; a script checked every quote word for word against the original message or file (821 of 821) and every "Patrick said" against the message author (224 of 224); 8 Sonnet topic checkers merged them into 207 lessons, and a script checked the count (821 = 806 placed in lessons + 15 rejected) and every "already covered" quote against a frozen copy of today's rules; one Sonnet cold reader, who saw only the finished register and the rules, raised 103 flags (all 474 of its quotes found), which shaped the folding above. The plan said to spot-check 1 in 10 quotes; the script checked all of them instead.
- **Agents:** 34 used (10 Haiku fetchers, 15 Haiku extractors, 8 Sonnet checkers, 1 Sonnet cold reader) against 28 planned. The first fetchers overflowed on long threads, so four more were needed, and the docs took more extractors than planned. No Opus or Fable.
- **The folding is my judgement.** Each proposal lists the rows it folds, and the 207 rows stand in `register.csv`, so you can check nothing was dropped.
- **Unsure:** the "recurred" flag was set by the cheaper models and was not checked against dates; "partial" and "covered" are rated unevenly across topics; about 22 rows are "partial" only because a memory note covers them, which agents spawned from a thread and other projects never read.
- **Untested:** none of the proposed scripts (the waiting page, the branch-overlap list, the tag maker, the test-source check) exists yet; each is a new tool that needs your yes.
