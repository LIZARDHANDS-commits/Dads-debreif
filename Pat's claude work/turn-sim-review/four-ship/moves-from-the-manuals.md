# Four-ship moves, as the manuals describe them (for Patrick to ratify)

Draft of 5 Oct 2026. Read-only research: nothing here is built or decided. Background is `design.md` in this folder (sections 3, 5, 6); where it records a ruling by Patrick I keep it and say so. Page references only, never manual text. SMM = Standard Manoeuvre Manual ch.16 (cited by section and paragraph); AFM7 and AFM8 = the March 2025 Four Plane briefs (cited by PDF page); Fig = SMM figure. I looked at SMM Figs 16.27, 16.29-16.35 and the brief pictures on AFM7 pp.14, 19, 20 and AFM8 pp.14, 20-22. The CFAFM was not looked for. The Orders (B2 ch 8) were not re-read; design.md finding 8 holds their limits.

**Words:** "Lead's frame" means left and right as Lead sees it. Speeds are KIAS. "Estimate" or "inferred" means the manual does not say; it is my reading, nothing more. **Directness tags:** DIRECT = the manual describes this move; IMPLIED = a manual gives it for the reverse, or for two aircraft, or says only "as in two-plane"; NOT IN MANUALS = no source at all.

**Altitude stack (Spread 4 and offset box, AFM8 p.14):** low to high #4, #3, Lead, #2; #2 plus 300 ft, Lead 0, #3 minus 300, #4 minus 600. Established once in position (AFM8 p.15, p.20); held while turning, vertical used for station keeping once rolled out, back to the stack promptly when Lead anticipates a turn (AFM8 p.14 items 3-5, p.17). The SMM's own example (Fig 16.33, text under it) differs: see question 4. No manual gives a stack for plain fighting wing; it is kept through a turning rejoin from Spread 4 or the offset box (AFM7 p.17; AFM8 pp.19, 25) and comes off near route in the finger rejoin (AFM7 p.18 item 2b). Crossing line abreast aircraft need 300 ft minimum (AFM8 p.30). Altitude is the main means of separation in Spread 4 manoeuvring (SMM 16.43 note under para 119; AFM8 p.18 item 6).

## 2. The moves (one row per Lead's call)

Close formation changes are called by Lead on R/T; a wingman who does not understand stays put and asks; anyone unsure calls FREEZE (SMM 16.32 para 85). Wingmen take care that the aircraft ahead is in position before completing a change (para 86). No manual gives heights for a crossunder, only "behind and below"; no manual gives speeds for station changes.

## 6. Questions for Patrick

One at a time; each has a working answer the tool uses until you answer.

**Q1. How far abeam is #3 in Fluid 4?** AFM8 p.20 text says 6,000 ft; its picture labels the gap 4,000-6,000 ft. Options: (a) 6,000 ft, the text, and the later spread in AFM8 pp.21-22 starts from 6,000 (recommended, and what design.md uses); (b) anywhere in 4,000-6,000 ft, flagged outside it; (c) yours. Working answer: (a).

**Q2. What does "OFFSET BOX LEFT, IN PLACE 90 RIGHT" mean?** AFM8 p.22 (left box) prints the same call as p.21 (right box), "in place 90 right", but its pictures end with the wingmen on the opposite side of their leads. Options: (a) treat it as a typo for in place 90 left, so the left box is the mirror of the right (inferred, recommended); (b) fly it as printed, wingmen flowing across behind their leads; (c) ask Dad first; (d) yours. Working answer: (a), flagged.

**Q3. Is offset box from finger a move we build?** SMM 16.41 para 110 allows "OFFSET BOX EAST/WEST" from fighting wing or finger but gives no steps; AFM8 only builds it from fighting wing through Fluid 4. Options: (a) only the AFM8 path, and finger goes through fighting wing (recommended); (b) also the SMM call, with Dad supplying the steps; (c) yours. Working answer: (a). (Same as design.md Q4.)

**Q4. Which altitude stack?** AFM8 p.14 fixes #4, #3, Lead, #2 low to high in 300 ft steps. SMM Fig 16.33 text lets #2 set the block, with #3 and #4 in the opposite block (its example 7,750, 8,000, 8,250, 8,500), and SMM 16.42 para 115 gives #4 only "a slight stack" when moving out from finger. Options: (a) the brief's stack always (recommended: it is the March 2025 brief, and V2.7 already flies it); (b) the brief's, with an option for #2 to set the block; (c) the SMM's; (d) yours. Working answer: (a). (Design.md Q2; not yet answered in its Patrick's answers.)

**Q5. Leaving Spread 4 or the offset box: direct to finger, or only via fighting wing?** The manuals give only the turning rejoin to fighting wing (AFM8 pp.19, 25) and say four-ship rejoins follow two-ship procedure (SMM 16.34 para 94), where line abreast rejoins go to fighting wing first (SMM 16.20 para 65). Options: (a) via fighting wing only (recommended: what the manuals describe); (b) also allow a direct "FINGER" rejoin from Spread 4, treated as two-ship style; (c) yours. Working answer: (a).

**Q6. Speeds for fighting wing 4, Fluid 4 and the offset box.** No manual gives them (only 220 for line abreast, SMM 16.18 para 49; 200 or 180 for rejoins, AFM8 p.11; about 230 for a wingover, SMM 16.17 para 47). Options: (a) your 200 outside line abreast for fighting wing and Fluid 4, 220 for the offset box, labelled estimates (recommended, design.md Q9); (b) 200 for all three; (c) 220 for all three; (d) yours. Working answer: (a).
