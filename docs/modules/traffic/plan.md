## Step 2. Refactor the flying layer (Patrick, 4 Oct 07:25Z)

Today four systems move the aircraft: the circuit rail, the PFL rail with scripted heights, free physics controllers, and a 1 s slide between them; three phase machines decide what comes next (`pf/reset/traffic-architecture/review.md`, sections 3-4). The refactor keeps one rule: **every aircraft rides a path built from the T-6's performance at the moment it is needed** (turn radius from bank and true airspeed, climb from excess thrust, glide from the configuration's glide ratio, shaped by the wind), and only one piece of code moves the aircraft. A height or speed is flown at a rate the T-6 can make, never written in. Dynamic flying is used only where the path can't be planned (the closed-pattern climbing turn). This replaces the old route.js split review (old Step 6). Each pull request is checked on screen from the default start. Before code is deleted or rewritten, the pull request lists what it did. Steps that change what the tool does get their spec section approved first.

- [ ] **PR 1. Core formulas and the flight-math list (no change to what the tool does).** Move the formulas Traffic copies, where the copies agree, into `src/core` (G and turn rate and radius from bank, wind vector, compass bearing, wrap 0-360, cross and along track), add one glide function (ratio and sink by height and configuration) for PR 3 to use, write the flight-math list in `docs/modules/shared/` (Shared plan Step 2, moved up by Patrick), and put the Moose Jaw numbers in one airfield file. The flight-math duplicates are listed in `pf/reset/traffic-architecture/flight-math-duplicates.md`.
- [ ] **PR 2. One way to move the aircraft, with the circuit on it.** One path follower is the only code that writes position, heading and bank; one phase machine replaces the three. The circuit rides today's wind-shaped path, unchanged. The speed-kind mix-ups are fixed here (IAS used as true airspeed in calm air, heading passed where track is meant), since they move the aircraft slightly. Check: the same circuit track on screen, no position step bigger than the aircraft's own movement, every aircraft lands on the runway. Bank, radius, roll and pitch (Patrick, 07:58Z): in every circuit turn the bank varies with the wind to hold the wind-shaped ground track, and the radius comes from that bank and the true airspeed (break 60° and 2 G, SMM 4.17 paras 38-42; final turn up to 45°, SMM 4.19 paras 43-48). Roll rate, pitch rate and pitch attitude fit what the aircraft is doing: roll rate is a setting with its source (today 45°/s, and 90°/s in the breakout, both unsourced), and pitch is worked out from the climb or descent. A number with no page is marked an estimate.  Rolls and pitch changes are smooth, with no jitter (Patrick, 07:58Z): each eases in and out at a realistic rate. The break starts at one point that moves smoothly with the headwind (about 2,000 ft past the threshold in a 10 kt headwind, later in more wind; SMM 4.17 paras 38-42, EFIG p.151, p.183). Today calm air starts the break 2,000 ft past the threshold, but any wind at all uses the old V6 point 3,818 ft past, so 1 kt moves the break about 1,800 ft (`src/modules/traffic/route.js:609`, `:745`; Patrick, 07:59Z). If the jitter comes from step-by-step physics, smoothing only the drawn bank and heading is fine (Patrick, 08:01Z); the flown numbers stay exact. One likely source to check first: the 3D view works out its own bank from heading change instead of using the sim's bank (`src/modules/traffic/view3d.js:98-134`). Checked on screen at calm and in a strong wind.
