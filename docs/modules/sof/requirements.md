## Weather and limits mean

For the SOF there is no flight model, so "realistic" means that the screen tells the same truth a pilot would read from the raw reports. Every answer (a ceiling, a visibility, a forecast change period, a limit, an alternate call) must come from reading the actual METAR or TAF the way the Canadian rules say, with the reason shown in words, and anything missing, old or unreadable must show as unknown, stale or failed and never as good. The numbers that set the picture are the home trigger (alternate required when home is forecast below 3,000 ft and 3 SM, or below 2,000 ft and 3 SM when staying inside the MTCA, over the flight plus one hour; Gen Book p.7), the alternate minima by approach type (Gen Book p.7: 400-1, 600-2, 800-2 and the "or above the approach minima" variants), the TAF group rules (TEMPO against alternate minima, PROB against landing minima, BECMG worst of; Gen Book p.7), GNSS-only alternate conditions (Gen Book p.7 note), the weather-limit and crosswind charts (Gen Book p.8 and p.10), and the training-rule weather limits (Gen Book p.9, p.11), all collected at `pf/manuals/weather-and-limits-numbers.md:9-64`. What is not a requirement: V6's own numbers (V6's 600-2 default, its 20 NM lightning radius, its 75/20/40/60 minute freshness times) are starting settings that Patrick and a current SOF can change, not answers; there is no tolerance and no pass/fail time. A test should check behaviours such as "a TAF with a TEMPO below the limit marks that wave as needing an alternate", "a stale METAR never reads within limits", "a failed refresh keeps the last report with its age", and "a missing cloud group reads unknown", not "within 30 seconds of V6".
