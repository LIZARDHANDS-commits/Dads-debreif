# AETCMAN 11-248: cockpit and formation sight pictures

Page and figure references to **AETCMAN 11-248, T-6 Primary Flying (13 Aug 2025)**, the USAF T-6 primary flying manual, for the Formation Sim's CT-156 cockpit and the outside details other pilots use as references (TS-156, V2.208). Dad sent the pages as pictures on 9 Oct 2026. What each figure shows is said here **in our own words**; no manual text or picture is in the repo. Where a page number was not in Dad's picture, the paragraph it sits under is given instead.

The manual is the USAF's for the T-6A. The CT-156 Harvard II is the same airframe, but its panel and paint may differ; the RCAF SMM, when we have its formation chapter, wins for the CT-156's own references.

**The windscreen the tool draws** (front eye, head straight ahead; degrees above or below the eye's level line along the nose): the bow band's lower edge at about +7.8°, the glareshield's rim at about -7.2°, so about 15° tall. A pilot reading the manual's pictures with the manual's own attitudes gets the same shape: about 2.5° nose low puts the horizon about 65 % up (the table's "2/3 ground"), and about 1.5° nose low about 58 % (near "half").

**Final approach, the open question.** The sim's attitude model (Patrick's flight number, not changed) puts the nose about +3.5° up on a 3° final at 100 KIAS, +2.6° at 105 and +1.9° at 110. The aim point (3° below the horizon) then sits about 5 %, 10 % and 15 % up the windscreen, against the table's half, half and a third. Level flight at 200 KIAS and a no-flap final can only both show "half way" if their two pictures sit at the same angle in the windscreen, and the sim's attitudes put them about 6.8° apart, so no cockpit shape can do both. The table implies a final attitude about 3° lower than level cruise; the sim's is about 3.8° higher. Likewise the final turn: the sim gives about -0.3° to -1.0° (110 to 120 KIAS at 1,000 fpm), the table about 2 to 3° nose low. Fig 2.7 stays the default.
