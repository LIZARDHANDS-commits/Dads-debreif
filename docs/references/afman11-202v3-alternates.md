# AFMAN 11-202 Vol 3: when a USAF base needs an alternate

The rule the SOF uses at the USAF T-6 bases (KDLF, KEND, KRND, KCBM, KSPS; plan Step 2c, part F). It is written here in our own words, with paragraph numbers only, from a decision chart and the paragraphs 4.16 to 4.17 that Dad gave on 7 Oct 2026 (neither is kept here). Chapter 4 of the manual wins over this note. The MAJCOM supplement (AETC) and each wing's local guidance can add rules and are not read yet. Navy fields (KNSE, KNGP) fly OPNAVINST 3710.7 and CNATRA rules, which are not covered here.

"Worst weather" means the worst of the forecast for ETA ± 1 hour, TEMPO groups included. "Prevailing" leaves TEMPO out.

## Can the destination be filed to?

1. It needs a compatible published approach. If it has none, it can still be used only with an alternate that has one, where the forecast (TEMPO included) at ETA ± 1 hour allows a descent from the MEA to an approach and a landing; otherwise pick a new destination. Without a compatible published approach, ask for an FTIP review (10.10).
2. Prevailing weather (not TEMPO) at ETA ± 1 hour must be at or above the lowest compatible approach minimums (4.15). If it isn't, the destination needs MAJCOM authority to file below minimums with supplemental recovery procedures; without that, the same alternate test as item 1 applies.

## Does it need an alternate?

3. An alternate is needed if the destination has no compatible instrument approach (4.16.1). Otherwise no alternate is needed only if both of these hold:
   - worst weather at ETA ± 1 hour is at least **2,000 ft ceiling above the field and 3 SM visibility** (4.16.2.1);
   - worst crosswind at ETA ± 1 hour is within the aircraft's limits (4.16.2.3).

   If either fails, an alternate is required. It may be dropped en route if the destination improves past these (note to 4.16.2). The helicopter rule (4.16.2.2) does not apply to the T-6. MAJCOMs may allow holding instead of an alternate for isolated destinations (4.16.3); not used for the training bases.

## Is an alternate suitable?

4. It is **not** suitable if any of these is true:
   - its weather at ETA ± 1 hour, TEMPO included (except TEMPO groups for thunderstorms, rain showers or snow showers), is forecast below **a ceiling of 1,000 ft or 500 ft above the lowest compatible approach minimum, whichever is higher, and a visibility of 2 SM or 1 SM above the lowest compatible approach minimum, whichever is higher** (4.16.4, 4.16.4.1). If the alternate has no compatible instrument procedure, the forecast must instead allow a descent from the MEA, approach and landing in basic VFR, and the list below does not apply (4.16.4.3);
   - every compatible approach needs an unmonitored NAVAID (4.16.5.1);
   - the field doesn't report weather (4.16.5.2);
   - every compatible approach is marked "Alternate Not Authorized" (4.16.5.3);
   - a note in the "IFR Alternate Minimums" section disqualifies the field or all its compatible approaches (4.16.5.4); NOTAMs and the IFR Supplement can also carry these conditions (note to 4.16.5).
5. GNSS approaches (4.17): an SBAS-equipped aircraft may file a destination and alternate that both have only GNSS approaches (4.17.1); a GNSS receiver with fault detection and exclusion (FDE) but no SBAS may use a GNSS approach at the destination or the alternate, not both, with a RAIM prediction (4.17.2); without FDE the alternate needs an approach that doesn't use GNSS (4.17.3). **Which of the three fits the T-6A at each base is still to come from Dad** (working answer: 4.17.2). A suitable alternate must also pass the aircraft's own guidance and MAJCOM guidance. Then file IFR, or IFR/VFR composite, with that alternate.

## What the tool does with it (working answer until part F is built)

- The home trigger at a USAF base is 2,000 ft and 3 SM on the worst weather at ETA ± 1 hour, TEMPO included (4.16.2.1). This is the same pair of numbers as Moose Jaw's local trigger, from a different book.
- A crosswind over the T-6's limit also calls for an alternate (4.16.2.3). The limit comes from the aircraft manual and the SOF's crosswind setting.
- An alternate meets 4.16.4.1 when its forecast at ETA ± 1 hour (TEMPO included, except thunderstorm and shower TEMPOs) is at or above max(1,000 ft, lowest compatible approach ceiling + 500 ft) and max(2 SM, lowest compatible approach visibility + 1 SM). The SOF reads it against the alternate's approaches as entered; an alternate with no approaches entered reads amber "Incomplete", never a tick (SOF-32).
- Every alternate needs its compatible approaches and their minimums, its "Alternate Not Authorized" marks and its weather reporting. These come from the US Terminal Procedures and the Chart Supplement, entered per field with a "checked on" date.
