# AFMAN 11-202 Vol 3: when a USAF base needs an alternate

The rule the SOF uses at the USAF T-6 bases (KDLF, KEND, KRND, KCBM, KSPS; plan Step 2c, part F). It is written here in our own words, with paragraph numbers only, from a decision chart Dad gave on 7 Oct 2026 (the chart is not kept here). Chapter 4 of the manual wins over this note. The MAJCOM supplement (AETC) and each wing's local guidance can add rules and are not read yet. Navy fields (KNSE, KNGP) fly OPNAVINST 3710.7 and CNATRA rules, which are not covered here.

"Worst weather" means the worst of the forecast for ETA ± 1 hour, TEMPO groups included. "Prevailing" leaves TEMPO out.

## Can the destination be filed to?

1. It needs a compatible published approach. If it has none, it can still be used only with an alternate that has one, where the forecast (TEMPO included) at ETA ± 1 hour allows a descent from the MEA to an approach and a landing; otherwise pick a new destination. Without a compatible published approach, ask for an FTIP review (10.10).
2. Prevailing weather (not TEMPO) at ETA ± 1 hour must be at or above the lowest compatible approach minimums (4.15). If it isn't, the destination needs MAJCOM authority to file below minimums with supplemental recovery procedures; without that, the same alternate test as item 1 applies.

## Does it need an alternate?

3. No alternate is needed only if both of these hold:
   - worst weather at ETA ± 1 hour is at least **2,000 ft ceiling and 3 SM visibility** (4.16.2.1);
   - worst crosswind at ETA ± 1 hour is within the aircraft's limits (4.16.2.3).

   If either fails, an alternate is required. The helicopter rule (4.16.2.2) does not apply to the T-6.

## Is an alternate suitable?

4. It is **not** suitable if any of these is true:
   - its worst weather is below the alternate weather requirement of 4.16.4.1. Thunderstorm, rain-shower and snow-shower TEMPO groups don't count against it here. **The 4.16.4.1 numbers are still needed from Dad, with their page.**
   - every compatible approach needs an unmonitored NAVAID (4.16.5.1);
   - the field doesn't report weather (4.16.5.2);
   - every compatible approach is marked "Alternate Not Authorized" (4.16.5.3);
   - a NOTAM or anything else disqualifies the field (4.16.5.3.4);
   - every compatible approach falls under the IFR alternate minimums section (4.16.5.3.4).
5. A suitable alternate must also pass the SBAS and equipment rules of 4.17, the aircraft's own guidance and MAJCOM guidance. Then file IFR, or IFR/VFR composite, with that alternate.

## What the tool does with it (working answer until part F is built)

- The home trigger at a USAF base is 2,000 ft and 3 SM on the worst weather at ETA ± 1 hour, TEMPO included (4.16.2.1). This is the same pair of numbers as Moose Jaw's local trigger, from a different book.
- A crosswind over the T-6's limit also calls for an alternate (4.16.2.3). The limit comes from the aircraft manual and the SOF's crosswind setting.
- An alternate whose 4.16.4.1 test can't be done yet (numbers not supplied) reads amber "Incomplete", never a tick (SOF-32).
- Every alternate needs its compatible approaches and their minimums, its "Alternate Not Authorized" marks and its weather reporting. These come from the US Terminal Procedures and the Chart Supplement, entered per field with a "checked on" date.
